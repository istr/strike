ADR-029 defines a peer as a container-egress trust contract: the destination a step container may reach, plus the anchor strike verifies it against. A registry deploy starts no method container, so a peer declared on the deploy step is bound to nothing -- it is collected by CollectPeers and sealed as declared, while nothing dials it and nothing observes it. That is a declaration without an adressee, and a sealed declaration that corresponds to no act is the binding-by-convention ADR-051 names as the defect in its own Context. The kubectl path masked this: it was the only deploy method that started a container and passed the step peers to it. Removing that path (ADR-054) left the field inert rather than newly broken. Capture units are a separate case and keep their peers: they do run containers, and rejectSSHPeers already guards them. The control plane's own dial targets are method fields, not peers.
