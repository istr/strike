ADR-029 defines a peer as a container-egress trust contract: the destination a step container may reach, plus the anchor strike verifies it against. A registry deploy starts no method container, so a peer declared on the deploy step is bound to nothing -- it is collected by CollectPeers and sealed as declared, while nothing dials it and nothing observes it. That is a declaration without an adressee, and a sealed declaration that corresponds to no act is the binding-by-convention ADR-051 names as the defect in its own Context. The kubectl path masked this: it was the only deploy method that started a container and passed the step peers to it. Removing that path (ADR-054) left the field inert rather than newly broken. Capture units are a separate case and keep their peers: they do run containers, and rejectSSHPeers already guards them. The control plane's own dial targets are method fields, not peers.

## Amendment 2026-10-25 at 013959b: the class, not one field

Found by the item-0158 analysis, read at 013959b, no build. Peers are one
member of a class: fields of #Step that only a step container consumes,
accepted on the two kinds that start none. On a deploy step the run path
dispatches before input resolution, spec hashing and the cache check, and
the deploy package reads no step field of that class: args, env, secrets,
workdir and forceRun are read by nothing; inputs are validated and become
DAG edges, so the deploy waits on producers whose outputs it never
receives; peers are collected and sealed as declared. On a pack step args
and env enter the spec hash and nothing else; inputs are resolved, hashed
and turned into edges but never delivered, because pack reads pack.files
only; secrets and workdir are read by nothing; peers are sealed as declared
although no container dials them. Provenance is already rejected on both
kinds, since neither declares a file output for it to lie in.

Operator decision of 2026-10-25: this item owns the whole class rather than
a new item; sibling closure is why the scope is the class.
